What It Means For You

  • If you do the weekly shop: import tariffs come off more than 100 products, from baked beans and chocolate to marmalade. Combined with June’s cuts on pasta, fruit juice and tuna, the government puts the saving at up to £21 a household a year — modest, and dependent on retailers passing it on.
  • If you are an unpaid carer who works: earning a penny over the weekly limit will no longer cost you the whole week’s carer’s allowance. Instead £1 tapers away for every £1 earned above it, to be confirmed in Wednesday’s Budget.
  • If you drive for work: the 55p-a-mile tax-free rate for the first 10,000 business miles becomes permanent, and the rate above that rises from 25p to 35p. The Treasury says about three million people benefit; fuel duty is also expected to be frozen again.

GEO Geopolitical

Israel Votes With Netanyahu and Eisenkot Neck and Neck

Polls opened this morning in Israel’s first general election since the 7 October attacks, with Benjamin Netanyahu in a near neck-and-neck race against the former military chief Gadi Eisenkot. Neither bloc is projected to reach the 61 seats needed for a majority, so weeks or months of coalition bargaining may follow.

Dive deeper
Voters choose parties for the 120-seat Knesset, not a prime minister, which is why a clear result tonight is unlikely. Eisenkot leads Yashar, a new centre-right party, at the head of a “change bloc” that includes Naftali Bennett, Avigdor Lieberman and Yair Golan; Mr Netanyahu needs the far-right and ultra-Orthodox parties again. Security dominated the campaign, and in its final days he was reported to have pressed for action against Iran, which he denies. One analyst expects a change of tone rather than direction: “A new prime minister is likely to calm things down,” said Ahron Bregman of King’s College London, adding that “any new government will reflect and mirror this reality” of a sharp turn to the right.

Tehran Talks End in Pledge as Houthis Fire at Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan agreed with Iran late on Monday “to continue the dialogue” on regional security, after their defence and intelligence chiefs met Iran’s president in Tehran. Within hours the Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at the Eastern Province and four drones. Brent eased to about $115 on the diplomacy.

Dive deeper
Riyadh sent its defence minister, Prince Khalid bin Salman; Pakistan its army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir; Turkey its intelligence chief, Ibrahim Kalin. Saudi Arabia said the aim was to “prevent the return of military escalation”. The difficulty is plain: the Houthis struck Riyadh’s airport this month, killing at least 13, and on Monday claimed attacks on oil pumping stations, while Egypt’s parliament authorised its military to help defend the kingdom. Pakistan and Qatar brokered June’s interim deal between Washington and Tehran, which collapsed. Oil reflects both pulls: Brent touched $117 on Monday, its highest since April, before slipping 0.57% to $115.15 in early Asian trade, and October has been the worst month for attacks on shipping around Hormuz.

Melania Trump’s One-City Truce Takes Effect in Occupied Oleshky

A local ceasefire began overnight in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky, on the Dnipro in Kherson region, a week after it was announced by the US First Lady. Both Kyiv and Moscow say it is holding. It is meant to let aid in and allow civilians out, with an evacuation planned for Friday.

Dive deeper
Russia’s defence ministry said it stopped firing at 01:00 GMT. Ukraine’s human rights commissioner, Dmytro Lubinets, confirmed it: “As head of the Ukrainian negotiating team, I confirm that the Ukrainian side is honouring its commitments.” Oleshky had 24,000 people before the war; Ukrainian estimates of those left vary, from about 1,700 in the town to 6,000 including surrounding areas, and residents describe cooking garden weeds. There has been no electricity, gas or water for three years. Some fear Russia will not let evacuees reach Ukraine, or will search their phones. The truce is narrow: the same day Russia struck Kyiv, killing a woman of 46, and claimed 222 Ukrainian drones downed.

Western Powers Warn of Famine in Sudan as Nyala Is Struck

The United States, the European Union and 30 countries, Britain among them, warned of an “alarming deterioration” in Sudan’s food security and urged both sides to lift impediments to aid. Hours later a drone strike on a market in Nyala, the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces’ de facto capital, killed at least 21 people.

Dive deeper
The joint statement, released by the State Department, warns that without immediate action the number of children with severe acute malnutrition “could exceed the projected 825,000”, and asks for access across front lines and by air into Darfur. The UN-backed hunger monitor said in June that 5.1 million Sudanese faced severe hunger, with an El Niño drought driving up prices. A medic at Nyala Hospital said 21 were killed and more than 40 injured on Monday evening; residents said the market was frequented by RSF fighters. The army regularly strikes the city, where the RSF has declared a rival administration. Washington said last month that neither side represents legitimate government.

Jordan Strikes Syria After Karak Church Attack; Border Shut

Jordanian jets struck what Amman called “terrorist organisations” in Syria’s Daraa province, retaliating for Friday’s shooting at a Christian funeral in Karak that killed nine. Syrian health officials said five people, including women and children, died. Damascus says it was not notified and has closed Nassib, the only passenger and commercial crossing between the two countries.

Dive deeper
The two accounts diverge on the essentials. Jordan says it coordinated with the Syrian government and killed and wounded “several terrorists”; Syria says it was not told and that civilians, including an elderly man, were among the dead. Damascus said the closure means “the complete suspension of passenger and commercial traffic in both directions” while it investigates. Complicating Amman’s case, its interior minister, Mazen al-Faraya, said the three men arrested over the church attack — two brothers and their brother-in-law — were all Jordanian, found hiding in a village near the scene. Thirty-four were injured in the attack on the Greek Orthodox funeral.

UK UK Domestic Politics

Healey Cuts Food Tariffs and Freezes Fuel Duty Before Budget

The chancellor, John Healey, has unveiled five “everyday fixes” ahead of Wednesday’s Budget, removing import tariffs on more than 100 food products and making the 55p-a-mile business mileage rate permanent. Fuel duty is expected to be frozen again. “We can’t control global events, but we can act where we can make a difference,” he said.

Dive deeper
The measures are deliberately small, and the Treasury frames them that way: Mr Healey calls them “just a small part” of giving families breathing space, blaming conflict in the Middle East and Europe for higher everyday costs. The tariff package is the second this year; with June’s, the government expects consumer savings of roughly £245m to £595m a year. Other fixes extend two weeks’ paternity pay, worth up to £194 a week, to self-employed fathers, and add Post Office drop boxes. Treasury insiders warn the Budget itself will be low-key, with hard tax and spending choices deferred to next year’s spending review — a source of unease among Labour MPs who expected bigger change from Andy Burnham.

Carer’s Allowance Cliff Edge to Be Scrapped

The rule that strips unpaid carers of their whole weekly carer’s allowance for earning a penny over the limit is to be replaced by a taper, with £1 withdrawn for every £1 earned above it. The change follows years of reporting on carers left with debts running into thousands of pounds.

Dive deeper
The arithmetic of the old rule was brutal. The earnings limit is £204 a week and the allowance £86.45; a carer a penny over for a year would breach it by 52p yet owe £4,495.40. Overpayment debts typically ran to £2,000–£5,000 and in some cases £20,000, and some carers were convicted of fraud. An independent review by Liz Sayce, published last year, found hardship on a huge scale. The taper removes the penalty for working an extra shift or taking a pay rise It does not, however, raise the limit itself.

Alex Chalk to Lead Independent Review Into Henry Nowak Murder

The home secretary, Shabana Mahmood, has ordered an independent review into the murder of Henry Nowak, the 18-year-old student stabbed in Southampton last December, to be led by the former lord chancellor Alex Chalk KC. “We must get to truth of what happened before, during and after that awful night,” she told the Commons.

Dive deeper
The case drew national scrutiny after police bodycam footage showed the teenager handcuffed as he lay dying, having told officers he was injured. The review runs in two phases. The first examines police, health services, councils, schools and faith organisations for missed chances to intervene; Mr Chalk said it would ask whether reports were held back out of a “misplaced sense of cultural sensitivity”. The second waits for the police watchdog’s own investigation and will examine its conduct too. Initial findings are due within five months. The family welcomed it, saying they had “fought tirelessly for answers to questions no family should ever have to ask”.

Be Ready Call Drives Surge in Radio and Tinned Food Sales

Retailers report sharp rises in emergency purchases since the government urged households to prepare for crises. Iceland saw a 40% jump in tinned goods over the weekend; Robert Dyas a 160% rise in radio sales; Maplin a near-fourfold increase in torches. The defence secretary called it “encouraging that people are taking practical steps to be ready”.

Dive deeper
The campaign began on 22 October, when Wes Streeting advised three litres of water per person per day, tinned food, baby food, battery torches and power banks, citing threats from cyber attack to extreme weather. Iceland says its supplies remain healthy. Mr Streeting stresses the mundane: essentials help “whether that’s flooding, power cuts or burst pipes”. The timing lent it weight. Storm Boelo has left about 22,600 properties without power, two men were killed by falling trees, and the Met Office has urged people to keep torches and batteries ready.

Second DUP Figure Calls for O’Neill’s Removal as First Minister

The DUP assembly member Diane Forsythe has joined the MP Carla Lockhart in calling for Michelle O’Neill to be removed as Stormont’s first minister over her part in protests that blocked an Orange Order parade at Drumcree. “It is my view that Michelle O’Neill should be removed from office,” she wrote.

Dive deeper
The row is now inside the DUP as much as between parties. Ms Lockhart stepped down from the party’s officer board last week after its leader, Gavin Robinson, declined to withdraw the deputy first minister — the only route by which unionists could force Ms O’Neill from the joint office, since the posts stand or fall together. Ms Forsythe, also an officer, says “profound consequences” were promised and “must be honoured”. Ms O’Neill and other senior Sinn Féin figures have been interviewed by police over the Garvaghy Road protests; she says she will “continue to stand with the Garvaghy Road residents”.
