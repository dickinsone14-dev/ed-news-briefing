What It Means For You

GEO Geopolitical

Iran Offers Two-Day Hormuz Reopening if Washington Meets Its Terms

Abbas Araghchi says talks with Qatari mediators have produced a plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days, down from five, provided America lifts sanctions, cuts its regional forces and releases frozen funds. President Trump said talks were “going fine but you know they’re not very reliable”, while insisting one or two nuclear sites remained to “take care of”.

Dive deeper
Tehran’s conditions have not softened: the foreign minister wants the oil waivers restored, all frozen assets released — including $6bn held in Qatar — and recognition of the shipping routes it discussed with Oman. He called the American approach, a letter from Vice-President Vance, “a positive step” but said “we see no need to meet with the Americans at this stage.” Mr Trump, for his part, said “we can do that in 10 minutes”, naming the suspected Pickaxe Mountain complex, which Iran denies is nuclear. Central Command says the blockade has now redirected 144 vessels, up from 138 a fortnight ago. The secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council said no decision had been taken to change the nuclear doctrine, but that renewed war would reopen the question.

German Youth Protests Turn to Looting as France Marches Again

Cost-of-living demonstrations organised by the youth group Gen Z regelt turned violent overnight in Frankfurt, where shops were looted, a gun shop’s windows smashed and molotov cocktails thrown at emergency services. Police used water cannon in Cologne and Hamburg. In France, the interior ministry counted 320,000 protesters, 70,000 of them in Paris.

Dive deeper
The protests target school underfunding, spending cuts, the cost of living and the return of military service; the organisers’ spokeswoman, Lina Friedrich, said they were “against compulsory military service” and wanted “a fairer distribution of the burden”. Hesse’s interior minister, Roman Poseck, blamed “around 100 clearly identified left-wing extremists” and warned that “France must not be held up as a model”. Crowd figures diverge sharply: authorities in three cities reported up to 18,000, while the organisers claim 120,000 nationwide. In France the movement is shrinking — 320,000 compares with 550,000 on 17 October and 410,000 a week ago — but its spread across the Rhine is the new fact for European governments planning austerity and rearmament together.

Tel Aviv Rally Urges Eisenkot to Govern With Arab Parties

Thousands rallied in Tel Aviv urging opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to strike a deal with Arab-led parties to remove Benjamin Netanyahu. With all votes counted, Mr Netanyahu’s bloc holds 53 seats and Mr Eisenkot’s 48 — both short of the 61 needed — while Arab-led parties won a record 19. Together, Eisenkot and the Arab parties would command 67.

Dive deeper
Mr Eisenkot has said neither Arab-led party meets his conditions for partnership, and most of his bloc rejects relying on them. Likud has claimed, without evidence, that the Arab parties “stole” the vote with foreign help; Qatar calls the allegations “ridiculous and dangerous in equal measure”. Results are due to be certified and handed to President Herzog on Wednesday, and the new Knesset is sworn in on 10 November. Herzog then names the leader most likely to form a majority, with 28 days to try, extendable by 14. Failure means fresh elections with Mr Netanyahu as caretaker — which, with Iran talks unresolved, is the outcome markets fear: Tel Aviv’s benchmark index fell 4.1% on the week.

Tens of Thousands Rally in Delhi Demanding Election Chief Quit

Rahul Gandhi is leading a Congress rally at Ramlila Maidan demanding the resignation of the chief election commissioner, Gyanesh Kumar, over a voter-list revision that has removed 130 million names from draft rolls. The Election Commission says it is purging the dead and those who have moved; the opposition calls it “vote chori” — vote theft.

Dive deeper
The row deepened in September when the Indian Express reported that Mr Kumar took key decisions on the revision over the objections of his two fellow commissioners, raised on more than a dozen occasions; the commission says differences of opinion are normal. On Saturday opposition leaders staged a symbolic “impeachment” at Mallikarjun Kharge’s residence, accusing Mr Kumar of “treason against the nation and the Constitution of India”. It has no legal force: the commissioner can be removed only by Parliament. Prime Minister Modi said “some people” wanted to create chaos to defame him. The Gen Z-led Cockroach Janta Party has pledged support, giving the rally a generational edge that echoes Europe’s streets.

Binance Covers 90% of Losses as FBI Blames North Korea

The FBI said on Saturday that North Korea was responsible for the $6.3bn theft from Binance, naming the Lazarus Group, also known as TraderTraitor. The exchange says it has replenished 90% of the stolen assets from its own treasury and other funds, and remains on track to reopen withdrawals today.

Dive deeper
“Binance will absorb the financial impact of these stolen funds, not our users,” the company said; it has set aside more than $6.34bn and warns the last 10% may take longer to source. Around $3bn had already been frozen with law enforcement and analytics firms. The theft is the largest on record, eclipsing the $1.5bn Bybit heist of 2025, also attributed by the FBI to North Korea, which has always denied involvement. Co-chief executive Richard Teng says more than one person was involved and the attackers used a “self-destruct mechanism” to hide their tracks. The reason it belongs in a geopolitical column: stolen cryptocurrency is a principal funding stream for Pyongyang’s weapons programmes.

DOM UK Domestic Politics

Reeves Says Britain’s Future Lies in the European Union

In her first major intervention since leaving the Treasury, Rachel Reeves writes in the Sunday Times: “I do believe Britain’s future lies in the European Union.” She offers no timetable and does not say whether a referendum would be needed. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said any change would require a mandate at a general election.

Dive deeper
Reeves wants confirmation of a youth mobility scheme at the forthcoming UK-EU summit and “progress towards entry” into the EU’s SAFE rearmament fund. She follows Sir Keir Starmer, who told the British-Irish Chamber of Commerce in Dublin on Friday that “a stronger Europe is vital and the UK must be at the heart of it”, calling the shift in American policy “a permanent change of affairs, not an aberration of the Trump presidency”. Two former holders of the top two offices now argue openly for a European turn the 2024 manifesto ruled out — no free movement, single market or customs union. The Conservatives say there is no desire to rerun the debate; Nigel Farage says there is no mandate.

King to Address Slavery With ‘Sincere Sorrow’ as Apology Row Grows

The King will speak with “unflinching honesty” and “sincere sorrow” on the transatlantic slave trade when he addresses Commonwealth leaders in Antigua on Tuesday, Buckingham Palace says. The Sunday papers lead on Mr Burnham’s openness to a formal apology, the Express accusing him of letting the King down.

Dive deeper
The Palace was told in advance of the prime minister’s article and has made no changes to the speech; a spokesperson said it would “not be appropriate” for the King to address the government’s position. Britain has never formally apologised for slavery or offered reparations, and Mr Burnham has ruled out the latter. The Mail on Sunday calls his intervention “ill-timed”, saying it has triggered fresh calls for billions; the Express urges him to stop “shooting from the hip”. The King has already spoken of the “suffering of the past” in the Bahamas and visited a slave-rebellion site in Guyana. Tuesday’s speech at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting will be read against Downing Street’s new line.

Healey Will Not Promise Equal Minimum Wage Before Election

The chancellor, John Healey, has declined to commit to paying 18 to 20-year-olds the full adult minimum wage before the next election, potentially breaking a 2024 manifesto pledge. “There is no question about whether or if, it’s a question of when,” he told the BBC. He also signalled readiness for deep departmental cuts.

Dive deeper
Labour promised to remove “discriminatory age bands”; the Budget instead gave younger workers the same rise as everyone else, leaving rates of £11.27 and £13.21. Mr Healey framed the delay as a jobs question, calling a million young people out of work “a moral shame on this nation”. Unions and the party’s left are likely to object. Pressed on whether some departments face cuts of more than 2% a year, he said: “We will carry that through to the 10-year plan and the spending review next year.” The Institute for Fiscal Studies questioned this week whether those real-terms reductions will happen; Kemi Badenoch, in the Sunday Telegraph, says unfunded promises have left a “colossal” black hole, which the Treasury denies.

Healey to Veto Offshore Wind Farms That Cannot Lower Bills

New offshore wind projects bidding for subsidy contracts will be signed off only if the grid can absorb their power without pushing up consumer costs, the chancellor says. “There’s no sense building power that can’t connect and then paying to switch it off,” he wrote in the Times.

Dive deeper
Mr Healey insists “this is not a retreat from clean power. It is the credible route to it”, but renewables experts read it, alongside more than £3bn of cuts to the energy department’s budget to fund defence, as a further step away from the 2030 clean-power target and Ed Miliband’s agenda. The National Audit Office warned in September that constraint payments to idle turbines could reach £7.8bn a year by 2030. Heat-pump subsidies were also trimmed in the Budget: about 60,000 a year are fitted in existing homes against a 2030 target of 250,000. The test comes at the next renewables auction, where the new grid condition will apply for the first time.

Mahmood to Set Out Settlement Rules With Care Workers Spared

The home secretary, Shabana Mahmood, will present her final decision on indefinite leave to remain to Parliament this week. The BBC understands social care workers already in Britain are likely to be exempt from a longer wait, while most other workers would need ten years rather than five before qualifying for settlement.

Dive deeper
The original proposal would have made some lower-paid migrants wait fifteen years and applied the change to people already here, which Angela Rayner, before rejoining the Cabinet as housing secretary, called “un-British”. The consultation drew 200,000 responses. The ten-year wait could be shortened for those with higher incomes or who contribute to their communities. The Home Office will not comment until the statement is made, so the details remain briefed rather than announced, and no start date has been given. The concession on care workers matters because the sector relies heavily on overseas recruits: a fifteen-year wait would have weighed on retention in a service already stretched.
