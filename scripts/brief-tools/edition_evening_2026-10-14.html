What It Means For You

  • If you drive a diesel or run a fleet: the International Energy Agency says the diesel squeeze will last into the second half of 2027, with retail prices already at records in most major economies and delivered prices into Europe liable to rise again in the coming weeks.
  • If you are flying to or through Saudi Arabia: Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport is closed until 17 October after Saturday’s strike that killed thirteen people, airlines have cancelled flights, and the Houthis today told civilians to keep away from Saudi airports.

GEO Geopolitical

Container Ship Sinks in the Strait of Hormuz as Attacks Surge

The Singapore-flagged GFS Pride sank after being struck by an unknown projectile on Tuesday; all 21 crew were evacuated unhurt. A second Singapore-flagged ship was hit today and reached safe anchorage. Singapore’s maritime authority called the attacks “unprovoked, unjustifiable and a breach of international law”. Iran says the vessel struck a mine on an “unauthorised” route.

Dive deeper
The maritime trade monitor recorded twelve vessels attacked between 5 and 11 October, six of them on Sunday alone, and Kpler puts crude exports through the strait at 8.5 million barrels a day in the week to 12 October — nearly 40% below pre-war levels, after a September rebound under American escort. Two Iranian officials told Reuters the new tactics were decided at the top, with one aim being to frustrate efforts to lower oil prices before the 3 November midterms. The Revolutionary Guards’ naval commander says Tehran is “counting down every moment” to sinking American warships; Central Command replied that he “has no navy”. Only one other ship had been reported sunk since the war began.

Houthis Vow More Strikes on Saudi Oil and Airports

The Houthis declared Saudi airports and oil facilities legitimate targets, a day after a strike on Aramco’s Dhahran headquarters started a fire that sources say spread across several floors — more than the “limited fire” the energy ministry described. The coalition says it intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh today. Sources say operations continue normally.

Dive deeper
The war has widened from the Red Sea into the kingdom itself: Riyadh’s airport has been attacked four times, and Saturday’s strike there killed thirteen, the deadliest in Saudi Arabia in a decade. Iraq’s prime minister, Ali al-Zaidi, used his first visit to Riyadh to promise that Iraqi territory would not be a “launching pad” for attacks — pointed, since Baghdad’s own inquiry found last month’s drone strike on the East-West pipeline came from Iraq. Inside Yemen the cost is mounting. The World Food Programme says 800,000 people are cut off from food aid, 600,000 of them in encircled Taiz; its country director warns this “can rapidly spiral into a humanitarian catastrophe”.

American Inflation Rises to 3.7% on the Eve of the Midterms

Consumer prices rose 3.7% in the year to September, above the 3.6% forecast and up from 3.4%, in the last reading before the 3 November elections. Petrol rose 9.1% in the month and accounted for more than half the increase; core inflation reached 2.6%. Treasury yields rose and Wall Street fell.

Dive deeper
This is the war arriving in the American shopping basket: energy is up 20.4% on the year and heating oil 74.2%, while food was flat in September. The core figure is what alarms bond traders — Christopher Rupkey of Fwdbonds called it a “shocking inflation report” and said “inflation is alive”. The ten-year Treasury yield rose to 5.242% and the two-year by more than five basis points. The Federal Reserve raised rates in September for the first time in three years and meets on 27–28 October; futures now price a further rise by December as near-certain. For Britain the transmission is through the dollar: sterling is down about a cent on the day.

Starlink Limits Disrupt Ukrainian Missions as 600,000 Lose Power

Ukrainian terminals moving faster than 160km/h lost connection overnight, wrecking a number of drone missions, according to Serhiy Beskrestnov, a presidential adviser: “It’s clear that this action cannot be a coincidence.” Elon Musk said the military service “is not under SpaceX control”. Russian strikes left at least 600,000 people without electricity.

Dive deeper
The speed threshold matters because Starlink-guided interceptors are Ukraine’s best answer to Russia’s jet-powered Shaheds; interception rates fell from above 90% to about 60% when those arrived. There are no reports of static terminals being cut, so front-line communications should hold. The Ukrainian air force says no warning preceded Tuesday’s first ballistic barrage, though “foreign partners” flagged three later ones — and the European Commission says it has “no indications” American intelligence sharing has stopped. Russia fired 39 missiles and 128 drones, killing three in Kyiv, with six more killed in Lutsk. Sergey Lavrov says Ukraine does not deserve the “respite” of Mr Trump’s proposed energy ceasefire.

Energy Agency Warns the Diesel Squeeze Will Last Into 2027

The International Energy Agency’s monthly report says gasoil supply will fall short of demand this quarter and lost stocks are unlikely to be rebuilt before the second half of 2027. Global diesel exports fell 700,000 barrels a day year-on-year in September. Wholesale prices touched record highs of about $220 a barrel in Europe.

Dive deeper
The agency’s point is that markets went into winter without a buffer: heavy inventory draws over the summer “deprived global gasoil markets of crucial stock cover ahead of the peak winter demand season”. A loss of 1.3 million barrels a day from Russia and the Middle East was only partly replaced. That is the backdrop to the American deal for Russian diesel, which the European Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis dismissed in Bangkok: the volumes are “not going to have any macroeconomic significance”. A second shortage is building behind it — a senior United Nations official warns that Asia and Africa face a food crisis unless Washington and Tehran agree a stalled plan to move fertiliser through Hormuz before planting season.

UK Domestic

Burnham Tells Zelensky Britain’s Support “Will Not Change”

Volodymyr Zelensky was met in Downing Street by a ceremonial guard — which Number 10 says it can find no record of for a foreign leader there — after an audience with the King. “We are behind you and that will not change,” the prime minister said, announcing faster delivery of British-made air defences. Mr Zelensky said he would raise Starlink and intelligence.

Dive deeper
The ceremony did the talking that the prime minister would not. At PMQs Sir Ed Davey invited him to call the Russian diesel deal “yet another shameful Trump betrayal”; Mr Burnham declined, saying his role was to “try and repair bridges so that the support comes back”. That is a deliberate bet that London is more useful as a broker than a critic, made after the American president said it was “time for Ukraine to get a new president”. Mr Zelensky said there had been “some difficulties” and “a lot of different surprises on which you can’t count”, and declined to say whether Washington had stopped sharing intelligence. Further military and financial packages are promised “in the coming weeks”.

Burnham Rules Out Partnership With Greens Over Zionism Motion

The prime minister told the Commons the Green Party’s conference motion defining Zionism as racism “is wrong, it is antisemitic, and this side of the House will have nothing to do with it”, adding that Labour “cannot support a partnership” with a party holding that position. Zack Polanski accused him of arming “a genocide”.

Dive deeper
The practical question is local. Labour governs in coalition with the Greens in Sheffield, Lewes and Warwick and alongside them in more than ten other councils, and Downing Street would not say whether the ban reaches that far. The motion passed by 1,022 votes to 813 — about 0.4% of a 230,000 membership — and was criticised by four Green MPs and two former leaders. Mr Polanski, who calls himself the only Jewish leader of a party, has said the wording was “imperfect” while defending its substance. The Green MP Ellie Chowns says policy made by so few members shows “something isn’t working” in the party’s structures. It is policy, but need not reach the manifesto.

Burnham’s Dinners With Man City Chairman Left Out of Statement

Deleted records show Andy Burnham dined with Khaldoon Al Mubarak at matches in 2017 and 2018 while mayor, hospitality valued at £300 and about £699. Neither was specified in Monday’s official account of his dealings with the club. Asked at PMQs whether the Premier League charges were raised with him, he said: “They did not raise those issues with me.”

Dive deeper
The dinners were declared at the time; the issue is the gap between a promise of “everything” and a statement Mr Burnham himself called his “key dealings”. The register containing them has since been removed from the combined authority’s website, which says it publishes only the current term’s entries — though its site still carries entries from 2022. His press secretary referred questions back to that statement. The shadow sports minister Louie French says the prime minister “cannot hide behind the GMCA”; Reform’s Robert Jenrick says “this saga stinks”. It matters because Mr Burnham said last month he would be “really concerned” if City’s owners sold up after the club was found guilty of breaking league rules.

IMF Singles Out Britain’s Deficit Plan Before the Budget

Kristalina Georgieva said Britain is “one country where action to reduce debt and deficit has been taken decisively”, the third such endorsement from senior IMF officials in a day. The fund lifted its growth forecast for this year to 1.3%. The chancellor is reported to be preparing a crackdown on VAT dodging through online marketplaces.

Dive deeper
Praise from Washington’s fiscal watchdog is also a constraint. The IMF finds only Britain in the G7 with a serious plan to cut annual borrowing — worth nearly 4% of national income — while America, Germany and Japan borrow more; the reward could be lower relative borrowing costs. It leaves John Healey little room on 28 October, and the Institute for Fiscal Studies warns the Budget risks being a “work of fiction” unless it covers £35bn of spending pressures. The marketplace plan, reported by The Times and said to raise up to £1bn, would make platforms collect VAT for sellers above £90,000 to fund business-rate relief. Amazon welcomes it; the Treasury will not comment.

Ministers Move Against Bogus Self-Employment Affecting 900,000 Workers

The government has opened a consultation on businesses that label staff as self-employed to avoid paying the minimum wage and holiday pay, citing estimates that up to 900,000 people are affected — among them couriers, delivery drivers and hairdressers. “When loopholes are exploited, people are denied their basic employment rights,” said Jonathan Reynolds, the business secretary.

Dive deeper
The package cuts both ways: the genuinely self-employed would gain a right to a written statement on each contract and wider protection against blacklisting. What it omits is the bigger promise. Labour committed in opposition to a single status of “worker” in employment law, and these proposals do not. Meri Ahlberg of the Joseph Rowntree Foundation calls them “a step forward” but warns that without single status and tax reform “it will still be cheaper to employ people in less secure jobs”, leaving millions without sick pay or parental pay. The incentive she describes is a tax one — a lever only the Budget can pull.

Davey, cheered after seeing off a threat to his leadership, asked the prime minister to condemn the American deal for Russian diesel. Mr Burnham would not, saying he should “be careful in terms of laying the ground to bring people back together”. He also promised further cross-party talks on social care “hopefully within the next month”.

Farage: Small Boats Are a National Security Emergency

Farage said unidentified men from Iran were arriving weekly. Mr Burnham began by acknowledging that the person accused of killing Ann Widdecombe has also been charged with planning an attack on Mr Farage — “I defend his right to say it, and I will work hard to secure his safety” — then said crossings “are coming down” and removals are up.

Baldwin: Rule Out Any Deal With the Greens

Baldwin (Conservative) asked the prime minister to rule out co-operation with the Green Party after its Zionism motion. He condemned the motion “unequivocally” as antisemitic and said Labour could not support a partnership with a party holding that position.

Manchester City: “They Did Not Raise Those Issues With Me”

Pressed on whether anyone at the club raised its Premier League charges with him, Burnham said they had not, and that he was happy for “everything to be set out in full” — while describing the published statement as a record of his “key dealings”.
