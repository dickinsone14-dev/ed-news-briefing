Evening Briefing

Wednesday 25 November 2026 — 17:55 GMT

What It Means For You

  • Poundland: all shops and distribution centres stay open and keep trading while administrators at AlixPartners seek buyers, but the chain is expected to be broken up, with store closures and job losses likely.
  • Car buyers: an Irish proposal would let Britain join the EU’s “Made in Europe” scheme without matching EU tariffs on Chinese electric cars — alignment that would have pushed up the price of those increasingly popular models in the UK.
  • Nuclear test veterans and their families: an independent panel will deliver an interim report by 3 October 2027, and the Prime Minister says a full apology, and possibly compensation, will follow.

GEO Geopolitical

Washington proposes new tariffs on China, the EU and twelve others

The United States has proposed fresh Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners, including an extra 20% on Chinese goods. The European Union faces a starting rate of 15%, which could rise to 20%; the duties are not expected before January. “When it all nets out, it really will just top up a few percentage points,” said Jamieson Greer, the US Trade Representative.

Dive deeper
The measures replace the “Liberation Day” duties struck down by the Supreme Court in February, using trade-practice investigations instead of emergency powers. Beijing called “so-called ‘overcapacity’” a “false proposition”; the European Commission rejected the tariffs, noting the 15% cap already agreed. Mexico and Norway were investigated but spared for now. Capital Economics judged the move “not as alarming as it looks”, since it largely replaces earlier levies. The US–China trade truce expires on 10 January; hearings on the new duties follow on 25 January.

Tech sell-off deepens as hot US inflation cements rate-hike bets

Chip stocks led a fresh slide on Wall Street on Wednesday: Nvidia fell 8% and AMD, Micron and Intel more than 9%, with the Nasdaq down nearly 4% at one stage. October’s PCE index showed inflation of 3.8%, core 3.3%, leaving markets fully priced for a Federal Reserve rise on 9 December. By 17:55 GMT the S&P 500 had pared its loss to 0.9%.

Dive deeper
The brief rally that greeted Jensen Huang’s reinstatement at Nvidia has evaporated, and Anthropic shares remain below their $83 debut price. Third-quarter GDP was revised to a 4.3% annualised rate, reinforcing the view that policy is not restraining demand. “Inflation is too hot and it will not be brought under control as long as economic demand is also too hot,” said Chris Rupkey of Fwdbonds. The VIX volatility gauge was up almost 12% this evening. US markets close for Thanksgiving on Thursday and reopen for a shortened session on Friday.

Ukraine says it hit five Russian jets at Savasleyka airbase

Kyiv’s General Staff said three MiG-31s and two Su-34s were damaged at Savasleyka, 300km east of Moscow, adding that “there may be even better news to come”. Ukraine also struck two refineries in Samara. Russian attacks overnight cut power in five Ukrainian regions, and the occupied Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant fell back on diesel generators.

Dive deeper
Savasleyka hosts the MiG-31s that carry Kinzhal hypersonic missiles, whose take-off routinely triggers nationwide alerts across Ukraine. “Did you repair them? We’ll hit them again,” Kyiv’s defence ministry said of the Samara refineries, some 800km from the front. Zaporizhzhia’s loss of external power was its 17th this year, according to Ukraine’s deputy energy minister. Vladimir Putin said on Tuesday that Moscow would “inevitably” retaliate for the refinery campaign, while insisting the fuel market had suffered no “critical damage”.

Iran rations electricity as blockade and gas shortages bite

Millions of Iranians face rolling power cuts of about two hours a day, imposed from Sunday, as the US naval blockade and strikes on energy infrastructure take their toll. President Masoud Pezeshkian said the attack on gas infrastructure meant “we should therefore expect difficulties this winter”. The rial has fallen to a record of more than 2.8 million to the dollar.

Dive deeper
A government spokeswoman warned the winter gas shortfall could reach 430 million cubic metres a day, against about 300 million in previous years; the national gas company says 11 of 31 provinces may struggle for supply. Pezeshkian has described a “silent economic war” that is “tougher than a war with missiles”. Military pressure has not translated into disarmament: the Institute for the Study of War and the Critical Threats Project report that the Revolutionary Guards have developed hybrid ballistic-missile variants and appear to be manufacturing them.

Israeli strike kills Hamas security chief and family in Gaza City

An Israeli air strike at dawn killed Mohammed Abu al-Ouf, head of Hamas’s internal security service in northern Gaza, with his wife and their two teenage daughters in Shati refugee camp. The Israeli military said he was killed “to remove the threat he posed”. It came a day after the service’s overall commander was killed.

Dive deeper
Abu al-Ouf was reportedly the likely successor to Naim Abu Nahal, who had led the service only since September after Israeli forces captured his predecessor. Israel accused him of rebuilding Hamas’s capabilities “in systematic violation of the ceasefire”; Gaza’s interior ministry called the strike a “heinous crime”. At least seven Palestinians were killed across Gaza on Wednesday, and at least 1,680 people have been killed during the ceasefire, which began in October 2025. Hamas has urged the ceasefire’s guarantors to intervene.

Poundland collapses into administration, putting thousands of jobs at risk

Poundland has called in administrators from AlixPartners after its US owner, Gordon Brothers, failed to agree a sale, putting between 11,000 and 12,000 jobs at risk before Christmas. Gordon Brothers said it had “explored every avenue”, but that Poundland remained vulnerable to inflation and any downturn in sales. AlixPartners will consider selling the business “in whole or part”; a break-up of the 36-year-old chain is expected.

EU draft would admit Britain to ‘Made in Europe’ without EV tariffs

Ireland, which holds the EU’s rotating presidency, has proposed letting Britain join the bloc’s “Made in Europe” manufacturing scheme without matching EU tariffs on Chinese electric cars. France and Germany had wanted alignment as a condition. Burnham told MPs the Government had “removed the threat” the scheme posed to British industry.

Dive deeper
The scheme, formally the Industrial Accelerator Act, would restrict goods from outside the bloc, with China and the car industry chiefly in view. Under the Irish draft, Britain, Switzerland and EEA states would have to open their subsidy and procurement schemes to EU firms. It must pass EU ambassadors, competition ministers next month, the Commission and the European Parliament. Pressed on whether Burnham had overstated progress, No 10 said it engaged “in a genuine spirit of cooperation”. Michael Ellam, the official who negotiated last week’s UK–EU deal, will step down at the end of the year.

Burnham promises nuclear test veterans an apology by October 2027

The Prime Minister told the Commons that the independent panel on Britain’s Cold War nuclear tests will deliver an interim report by 3 October 2027, the 75th anniversary of the first test. Dive deeper
Burnham drew the parallel with the Hillsborough independent panel, recalling David Cameron’s apology from the despatch box. Pressed by Kemi Badenoch and Sir Ed Davey on compensation, he said: “We should go wherever the truth take us and we should make whatever recompense is needed as a result.” The Ministry of Defence has for decades denied a cover-up over radiation exposure. Veterans of tests in Australia and the South Pacific in the 1950s and 1960s say they suffered more illness, and their descendants hereditary conditions; the campaign comes 14 years after a Supreme Court challenge failed.

Burnham insists he is ‘listening to Wales’ amid devolution row

Andy Burnham told MPs he is “listening to Wales”, a week after rejecting calls to devolve justice, policing, rail infrastructure and the Crown Estate to the Senedd. Challenged by Plaid Cymru’s Ann Davies, he offered deeper devolution of employment support instead, saying: “I will never completely rule things out, but at this moment in time that is the issue that we should work on.”

Dive deeper
Davies accused the Prime Minister of overruling the mandate of May’s Senedd election and of ignoring 22 Welsh councils, TUC Cymru and two former Welsh Labour first ministers, Carwyn Jones and Mark Drakeford. “When it comes to England, he believes power should be pushed out of Westminster. When it comes to Wales, he is determined to keep it in,” she said. The charge is pointed for a Prime Minister who made his name championing devolution to English regions.

Wednesday 25 November 2026 — 14:51 GMT

Saudi-led coalition strikes Hodeidah as Houthis threaten Red Sea ports

The Saudi-led coalition said it had struck “operational capabilities” at the Houthi-held port of Hodeidah and sites in two northern governorates, after telling civilians to evacuate. Yahya Saree, the Houthis’ military spokesman, warned that “the port of Jeddah, along with all other Saudi enemy ports along the Red Sea coast” would become targets. The strikes followed Tuesday’s Houthi attack on the PIF Tower in Riyadh, which injured 11 people.

Dive deeper
The coalition spokesman, Turki al-Maliki, called the Riyadh strike a “cowardly terrorist attack”. The escalation comes as Houthi fighters, having swept aside government forces in September, mass to the north and east of Aden, the Saudi-backed government’s temporary capital; residents and aid agencies are preparing to evacuate. A wider Saudi–Houthi war would put Red Sea shipping, already disrupted by the Iran conflict, under further strain. Brent rose 1.7% on Wednesday.

Asian markets fell sharply as doubts about the AI boom overwhelmed the rally that followed Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia’s chief executive. South Korea’s Kospi plunged 6.2% and Taiwan’s Taiex closed 4.8% lower, having been down more than 8% earlier. The Nikkei fell 2.8%. In New York, the S&P 500 was down about 3% in early trading and the Vix volatility index jumped almost 30%.

Dive deeper
Chip-equipment makers took the heaviest losses: Tokyo Electron fell 12.5% and Advantest 12.9%. The rout spread to safe havens and crypto: gold fell about 3% and bitcoin 3.5%, while US and UK government bond yields eased as investors moved into sovereign debt. London held up better, with the FTSE 100 only marginally lower because it has little exposure to the AI trade. The question for markets is whether this is a correction in crowded technology positions or the start of a wider reassessment of AI capital spending.

Leaked Russian data show almost 500,000 soldiers killed in Ukraine

At least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died since the 2022 invasion, according to leaked Rosstat records analysed by BBC Russian and Mediazona. The data, from more than 11 million death records hacked by Belarusian and Ukrainian groups, show that 65% of Russians aged 18 to 24 who died in 2025 were killed in the war. Rosstat admitted a cyber-attack but denied that protected information had leaked.

Dive deeper
The analysts matched 90% of the leaked records against the BBC’s own open-source list of 290,012 confirmed deaths. Military experts estimate that list captures 45–55% of the true toll, implying between 527,295 and 644,471 dead. Among Russians aged 25 to 34, 59% of deaths in 2025 were war-related. Only among those aged 45 to 54 does cardiovascular disease kill more Russians than the war. Moscow does not publish casualty figures. CNN analysis puts the cost of Russia’s slow advance in Donbas at “at least 40,000 dead and injured a month”.

Sybiha tells Putin to “stop complaining” over refinery strikes

Andrii Sybiha, Ukraine’s foreign minister, told Vladimir Putin to “stop complaining” about attacks on Russian refineries, saying he could end them by ending the war. On Tuesday Putin told oil executives in the Kremlin that Ukraine was still striking refineries despite Donald Trump’s appeal to stop. “Retaliatory action from our side is inevitable,” Putin said, according to Tass.

Dive deeper
In September Trump urged Volodymyr Zelensky to stop targeting Russian refineries, saying the attacks were “hurting the world” at a time when supply disruption from the wars in Ukraine and Iran had pushed US diesel prices to a record. Kyiv has since continued the campaign, treating Russia’s fuel and export revenues as a legitimate economic target. Putin’s threat of retaliation points to further Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure as winter sets in.

Huawei convicted of stealing US trade secrets but cleared of racketeering

A federal jury in Brooklyn found Huawei guilty of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and of bank fraud, but acquitted it of the more serious overarching racketeering charge. Over the two-month trial, prosecutors accused the Chinese technology company of lying, cheating and stealing to gain a competitive advantage. Huawei’s defence argued that its success came from its own innovation.

Dive deeper
The verdict is a partial win for both sides. Prosecutors secured convictions on core counts in a landmark case against one of China’s most important companies, but the racketeering acquittal rejects their central claim that Huawei operated as a criminal enterprise. Huawei’s lawyers had argued that prosecutors decided the company was a criminal organisation and built their case from there. The ruling lands at a sensitive moment for US–China technology relations, with chip stocks already under heavy pressure.

UK UK Domestic Politics

Energy bills to rise 26% from January as Iran war lifts gas prices

Ofgem said the price cap would rise 26% in January, adding about £37 a month to a typical household bill. Sustained for a year, that would take annual bills from £1,723 to £2,166. Ofgem said the rise was driven entirely by higher wholesale costs, mainly because of the Iran war. “We are staring down the barrel of a brutal winter,” said Dame Clare Moriarty, chief executive of Citizens Advice.

Dive deeper
“Conflict in the Middle East continues to drive energy costs higher, and we know this price increase will be a worry for many households as winter approaches,” said Neil Kenward, Ofgem’s director general for markets. He said the regulator had written to suppliers reminding them of their responsibilities to customers in difficulty. Charities and suppliers expect household debt to rise. The increase covers the coldest quarter of the year, when usage peaks, so it adds pressure on the government’s cost-of-living measures less than a fortnight after the Budget.

Fairfield review finds Met still racist, misogynist and homophobic

The Metropolitan Police remains institutionally racist, misogynist and homophobic three years after the Casey review, according to a report by Dr Gillian Fairfield. She found the force had not made good progress in any of the areas flagged for improvement. “None of these behaviours have changed sufficiently. Neither has the Met’s institutional defensiveness,” the review said. Sir Mark Rowley, the commissioner, accepted all ten recommendations.

Dive deeper
Dr Fairfield said that after several strategic briefings with the Met, no senior leader had used the word “racism”. “If it cannot name it, it cannot face it. The leadership team does not currently do this,” she said. The review found the Met still treats racism as “something people can be trained out of” rather than a structural problem. Rowley said prejudice had “got to be ruthlessly confronted”. The findings will intensify pressure on the Met’s leadership and on ministers to tie reform to measurable outcomes.

Burnham brings forward NDA curbs and promises age-of-consent consultation

Andy Burnham has brought forward changes so that from January, victims of crime or workplace harassment can speak to whoever they choose, even if they have signed a non-disclosure agreement. He called the move “just the start” and promised a consultation on a staggered age of consent. “We’ll only stop the violence when we end the silence,” the prime minister wrote in the Guardian.

Dive deeper
Burnham called recent high-profile rape trials a “watershed moment” and left open a wider ban on NDAs. The debate was reopened by allegations of improper behaviour against David Walliams, which he denies, and by Russell Brand’s conviction for rape and sexual assault. In both cases it has been suggested that women felt silenced after signing NDAs. The age-of-consent consultation is likely to be the more contentious of the two measures, and its terms will be closely watched.

Hillsborough-style panel to examine nuclear test veterans’ blood tests

Andy Burnham has set up an independent panel into the blood-testing of service personnel during Britain’s nuclear weapons trials, which he called a “criminal cover-up on an industrial scale”. James Jones, the former bishop of Liverpool who chaired the Hillsborough panel, will lead it. The Ministry of Defence has admitted carrying out biological examinations on exposed troops, having denied it to Parliament and the courts for decades.

Dive deeper
About 22,000 British military personnel took part in 45 large-scale nuclear weapons tests in Australia and the South Pacific between 1952 and 1967. In September the former defence minister Tobias Ellwood admitted unwittingly misleading Parliament in 2018 about the existence of the veterans’ medical records. The panel will follow the Hillsborough model and aims to publish an interim report in time for the 75th anniversary of the first UK nuclear weapons test. Veterans’ campaigners have long sought access to the records to support compensation claims.

£100 energy payment to reach 20 million homes from 4 January

More than 20 million households in council tax bands A to D will begin receiving a £100 energy payment from 4 January, the Treasury said as it set out how the Budget measure will work. The 13 million who pay council tax by direct debit will be paid automatically; others will be offered a bank payment or a council tax credit. Researchers warn that the payment covers only the first 11 days of January bills.

Dive deeper
The timing is awkward for ministers. On the same morning, Ofgem confirmed that a typical household will pay about £62 a week for energy from January, so the rebate is overshadowed by the 26% rise in the cap. The payment comes on top of the VAT cut on electricity and the £150 warm home discount for six million households on benefits, which campaigners want extended. Because councils administer the payment, it will depend on local authority systems working smoothly in the first week of January.
