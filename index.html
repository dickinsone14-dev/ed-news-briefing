Russian missiles crash in Poland during one of the war’s largest attacks

Two Russian cruise missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in south-eastern Poland overnight. They were part of what Volodymyr Zelensky called “one of the largest attacks” of the war, which killed eight people in Ukraine. Donald Tusk called it “an intentional provocation”, timed to coincide with Western exercises involving 11 Coalition of the Willing nations. Nato scrambled jets in response; nobody in Poland was hurt.

Dive deeper
Poland’s operational command said Russia fired 26 Kalibr and 72 Kh-101 cruise missiles and more than 500 drones. In Kyiv, ballistic missiles damaged the Podilskyi, Darnytskyi and Southern bridges, and nuclear plants were forced to cut output, causing power cuts in the capital and ten regions. Mark Rutte, Nato’s secretary general, called the incursion “clearly dangerous” and visits Warsaw on Saturday. Wes Streeting, the Defence Secretary, who is in Poland for the exercises, called the incursion “very serious”.

Iran is preparing to send tankers through the American naval blockade. “The blockade must be broken,” Abbas Araghchi, the foreign minister, said. Dive deeper

The blockade, reinstated on 14 July, has halted Iran’s seaborne crude exports since mid-July; TankerTrackers said one tanker was loading Iranian crude from another vessel in the Gulf. On Friday afternoon a tanker leaving the Strait of Hormuz was hit by an unknown projectile about eight nautical miles north of Khasab, in Oman, which started a fire; no casualties were reported. At the Brussels summit, EU and British leaders told Iran to stop “its hybrid attacks on European soil”.

UN court rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice ruled by nine votes to six that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority — the first time an international court has found a state guilty of genocide. Yuji Iwasawa, the court’s president, said Myanmar had the “intention to destroy” a substantial part of the group and engaged in “extensive destruction of evidence”. Myanmar must allow refugees to return and pay compensation.

Dive deeper
The case, brought by The Gambia, centred on the 2017 “clearance operations” in Rakhine State. The court found that about 10,000 Rohingya were “violently killed” and some 815,000 targeted; more than 700,000 fled to Bangladesh, where around a million refugees now live at Cox’s Bazar. It held that “genocidal intent is the only reasonable inference” from the evidence. Compensation will be set in later proceedings. The judgment lands as the court weighs genocide allegations linked to Gaza and Ukraine.
