Nepal Floods Kill 157; Hundreds Missing Include 33 Britons

Flash floods triggered by a landslide that dammed the Bhote Koshi River killed at least 157 people in Nepal and three in Tibet, with 579 tourists unaccounted for — 466 of them foreign nationals from 28 countries, including 33 Britons. A tour firm said it cannot reach a group of twelve Britons, aged 13 to 65, booked on a Mount Kailash tour. Mr Burnham said the UK “will do everything we can” to support those affected; helicopters have been unable to land in the gorge terrain.

Dive deeper
The mechanism was as sudden as it gets: a landslide walled up a Himalayan river until the dam burst, sending a wall of water through valleys full of trekkers in peak season, on both sides of the Nepal–Tibet border at once. That is why the missing list reads like a departure board — Americans, Australians, Canadians, Malaysians, Germans — and why the toll is still unsettled, with communications down and six hydropower stations damaged. The US Geological Survey's finding that the seismic reading was the landslide itself, not an earthquake, will matter for understanding whether warning was ever possible. For dozens of British families tonight, the questions are immediate rather than geological. Watch the toll, the fate of the Kailash group — and how quickly rescuers reach the cut-off valleys.

The Supreme Court challenge in London now carries stakes on both sides of the Atlantic. Watch the ruling — and whether other allies follow the Anglo-American lead.

Dive deeper

The conditionality is the point, though — Iran is converting the strait from a closed fist into a tap it can adjust, pressuring Washington to deliver sanctions relief without firing a shot. Both things are true, which is what makes the policy hard. The comparison with cytisine, the NHS’s newly adopted quit pill, will matter for prescribing; the long-term unknowns of vaping remain honestly unknown. Watch whether the NHS shifts its cessation services towards recommending vapes first — and whether the youth-protection agenda leaves room for it.
