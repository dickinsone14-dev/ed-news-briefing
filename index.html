Wednesday 23 September 2026 — 08:10 BST

What It Means For You

Three Hours of US-Iran Talks in New York; Tehran Names Its Price

The first American-Iranian contact since June’s memorandum collapsed took place on the UN sidelines: Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner spent about three hours with Iranian officials — “not the highest level, but it’s something”, in Mr Trump’s words, and “a very good meeting”. Tehran confirmed that Abbas Araghchi met Mr Witkoff through Qatari mediators and set its terms: the naval blockade lifted, frozen assets released, the war ended on every front. Brent closed below $100 for the first time since 8 September.

Dive deeper
Accounts of the room differ — the president called it a meeting, Mr Witkoff described mediators “who shuttled between the two sides throughout the day”, and Iranian television said its minister attended “at the insistence of the American side” — and the discrepancy is itself the diplomacy: each capital narrating the contact in the form its hardliners can bear. Iran’s three conditions are its opening bid, the seven-day Hormuz reopening is the good it is selling, and Mr Trump’s remark to the Gulf leaders that the war “could end right after the midterm elections, maybe before” is the clock. The strait tells the truer story: three commodity vessels transited on Tuesday against a ten-day average of fifteen, even as Saudi Arabia restarted its East-West pipeline. Watch whether the promised second round has a date by Friday, and what Pezeshkian says from the podium today.

Zelensky Offers an Energy Ceasefire After Trump Talks; Rubio Meets Lavrov Today

Forty minutes with Mr Trump produced an offer rather than an answer: “We are ready for energy ceasefire… if they will not attack our energy system, our electricity, heating system, water supplies,” Mr Zelensky said, adding that the president had not asked him to stop striking refineries. Kyiv proposed swapping the 600 Patriot missiles Germany will deliver in 2027-28 for American stock now — “they are positive to help us, but I didn’t get the very concrete answer”. Marco Rubio meets Sergei Lavrov in New York this morning.

Dive deeper
The energy truce is the first reciprocal formula Kyiv has put on the table since Washington began asking it to sheath the refinery campaign: pause the strikes Mr Trump himself called “a serious hit on the Russians… also a serious hit on the price of diesel” only if Moscow spares the grid before winter. Two things sharpen the moment — Mr Zelensky says intelligence shows Russia preparing “a new massive attack”, and NATO’s deputy commander, Air Chief Marshal Sir John Stringer, calls Putin “increasingly cavalier, dangerous and risk accepting” while urging the alliance to avoid “overreaction”. Russia’s own claims are fraying: its defence ministry falsely announced an encirclement at Dobropillya, and mobilisation drills in Udmurtia suggest the Kremlin is preparing to call up more men. Tuesday’s strikes killed eight Ukrainians. Watch the Rubio-Lavrov readout for the word “energy”.

Xi Lands in Washington Today as Beijing Draws Four 'Red Lines'

China’s leader arrives in Maryland today for his first American state visit since 2015, with Thursday’s summit and state dinner reportedly drawing Bezos, Musk, Altman, Cook, Pichai, Zuckerberg and Huang. A $14bn Taiwan arms package remains frozen; the trade truce expires in November; Scott Bessent has proposed an AI safety mechanism.

Dive deeper
The guest list is the tell: a summit staged for technology chiefs is a summit about chips, models and the terms on which two rivals let each other build intelligence — not about Taiwan, which Washington has quietly parked by holding the arms package in abeyance. Beijing’s red lines are old; publishing them in the People’s Daily the day before landing is the new part, a leader arriving with his refusals pre-announced so that no concession can be read as extracted. The November truce expiry gives both sides a deadline the summit will be measured against; the AI mechanism gives them something to sign. Senator Tammy Duckworth’s charge that the president “spends too much time sucking up to President Xi” previews the domestic reading of any warmth. Britain has a stake: Mr Trump reportedly agreed to raise Jimmy Lai at the Prime Minister’s request. Watch whether the arms package survives the visit.

Greenland Text Published: Golden Dome, No End Date, No Non-NATO Bases

The agreement signed at the UN was released overnight: it amends the 1951 defence treaty, allows “an additional Defence Area at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig”, permits the Golden Dome missile-defence system, bars military installations by non-NATO states, restricts non-NATO and non-EU investment in critical infrastructure and mining — and has no expiry. Mette Frederiksen called it “a deal that can last forever”; Mr Trump said no adversary would gain a presence “without our express written approval”. Both Copenhagen and Nuuk must ratify.

Dive deeper
Yesterday’s question — how “good for all three” and “permanent control” could share one document — now has an answer: the text grants permanence without sovereignty. Denmark keeps the flag; America keeps, indefinitely, the right to decide who else may build, invest or garrison on the island, which is control in every sense that matters to a strategist and none that matters to a constitutional lawyer. The investment clause is the sleeper — a veto over Chinese and other non-allied money in Greenland’s minerals, written into a defence treaty. Jens-Frederik Nielsen’s formulation, “not only an agreement about us, it is an agreement with us”, is the line Greenland’s parliament will have to believe; the text also keeps Greenland in NATO should it ever become independent. Watch the ratification debates in Copenhagen and Nuuk, and whether “forever” survives them.

Dive deeper

Watch whether the Gulf statement is echoed in the US-Iran channel, where an end to the war on “all fronts” is already Tehran’s demand.

Watch whether Gosport becomes a template for local protest at future landings.
